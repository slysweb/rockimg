What is a Pantone color?

Each Pantone number points to a fixed ink or dye formula. You send a code; a press or dye house mixes to that recipe instead of guessing from a JPEG.

Pantone LLC built the system to stop “your red / my red” fights in print. Graphic arts chips carry suffixes such as C (coated paper) and U (uncoated). Fashion, Home + Interiors chips use marks like TCX (cotton) and TPG (paper for interiors). Same family of numbers, different substrates—do not swap a C chip for a TCX dye lot and expect a match.

On a monitor you only see an RGB stand-in. Two calibrated displays can still disagree. For brand books, packaging, and apparel sampling, the physical fan deck is the contract. Pantone also names a Color of the Year; that is a marketing and trend cue, not a substitute for specifying a production code.

Graphic Arts vs Fashion, Home + Interiors

Graphic Arts (classic PMS) is for print and logos. FHI is for textiles and interiors. Read the suffix before you quote a number to a vendor.

PANTONE 185 C is a common example in packaging and logo work: coated stock, glossy ink. The uncoated sibling, 185 U, sits on a more absorbent sheet and looks slightly duller. Neither is a fabric standard. A mill asking for cotton will want an FHI TCX reference, not a C chip photographed on your phone.

If you only have a HEX from a website, you still have not specified a Pantone color. HEX is a screen recipe. You can hunt a nearby chip for inspiration, then confirm on a fan deck under the lighting your printer actually uses.

How to get Pantone color codes

Buy or borrow a current physical guide for production. Use Pantone Connect or Adobe libraries for drafting. Use an image extractor when you need a fast, unofficial shortlist from a photo.

Physical color guides

Official printed books remain the first choice for press checks and fabric labs. They fade, so shops replace them on a schedule. Cost is the tradeoff: one Solid Coated book already covers most logo work; fashion teams add FHI cotton decks. If a client signs off on a chip, write that exact code—including suffix—into the spec sheet.

Pantone Connect

Pantone’s official digital lookup sits behind an account. Designers use it to search names, convert between libraries, and drop chips into Adobe apps. Some libraries need a paid seat. Even then, the pixels on screen are still a preview. Connect does not replace a fan deck at the press.

Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop libraries

Illustrator and Photoshop ship with Pantone books in the swatches panel. Handy when you are drawing a logo or mockup. Treat those swatches as a drafting aid. Exporting a PDF with a named spot color is not the same as a lab-matched drawdown.

Screen RGB vs print reality

HEX and RGB describe light. Pantone formulas describe ink or dye on a specific substrate. A close HEX is still not a licensed match.

Uncoated paper drinks ink; coated paper sits on top. Cotton takes dye differently from polyester. Lighting at the press (D50 vs office LEDs) shifts what you think you approved. That is why shops insist on a current fan deck and, for critical jobs, a press proof or lab dip.

Use the online extractor to narrow the conversation—“these six chips are in the neighborhood of this photo”—then walk the client to the physical book.
